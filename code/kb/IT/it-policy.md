Policy Statement

In order to protect the security and integrity of Bennett University data, as well as to comply with
applicable laws and regulations, the access and usage of all systems and networks must be complied
with by all as per the various policies and guidelines.

Policy

The Bennett University policy regarding Abuse of Computers and Network Systems promulgate four
important principles of information technology: privacy of communications; integrity of data and
systems; intellectual property rights, and responsible use of network resources.

The use of computers or network systems** in no way exempts any member of the University community
from the normal requirements of ethical or legal behaviour in the Bennett University community. Data,
software, and computer capacity have value and must be treated accordingly. The use of a computer and
network system that is shared by many users imposes certain additional obligations.

The legitimate use of a computer or network system does not extend to whatever an individual can do
with it. Although some rules are built into the system itself, these restrictions cannot limit completely
what an individual can do or can see. In any event, each member of the community is responsible for
his/her actions whether rules are built-in, and whether they can be circumvented.

Members of the University community are expected to follow certain principles of behaviour in making
use of computers and network systems to respect, and observe policies and procedures governing:

« The privacy of or other restrictions placed upon data or information stored in or transmitted
across computers and network systems, even when that data or information is not securely
protected.

Violations include, but are not limited to

Accessing, or attempting to access, another individual's or entity's data or information without proper
authorization regardless of the means by which this access is attempted or accomplished; giving
another individual the means to access data or information they are not authorized to access;
obtaining, possessing, using, or attempting to use passwords*** or other information about someone
else's account; inspecting, modifying, distributing, or copying data, mail, messages, or software without
proper authorization, or attempting to do so; tapping phone or data lines; accessing files by
circumventing privacy or security restrictions. An owner's interest in proprietary software or other
assets pertaining to computers or network systems, even when such software or assets are not
securely protected.

Scope

This policy details specific requirements for the use of all computing and network resources at
Bennett University. Information resources and technology at Bennett University support the
educational, instructional, research, and administrative activities of the University, and the use of
these resources is a privilege that is extended to members of the Bennett University community. As
a user of these services and facilities, you have access to valuable University resources, legally
restricted and/or confidential information, and internal and external networks. Consequently, it is
important for you to behave in a responsible, ethical, and legally compliant manner.

In general, acceptable use means ensuring that the information resources and technology of the
University are used for their intended purposes while respecting the rights of other computer users,
the integrity of the physical facilities, and all pertinent license and contractual agreements. If an
individual is found to be in violation of the Acceptable Use Policy, the University may take disciplinary
action, including restriction of and possible loss of network privileges or more serious
consequences, up to and including suspension, termination, or expulsion from the University.
Individuals may also be subject to laws governing many interactions that occur on the University’s
networks and on the Internet. These policies and laws are subject to change as national and state
laws evolve.

Purpose

This policy applies to all users of computing resources owned or managed by Bennett University.
Individuals covered by the policy include (but are not limited to) University faculty and visiting faculty,
Staff, students, alumni, contractors, volunteers, guests, or external individuals and organizations
accessing network services via the University’s computing facilities.

Computing resources include all University-owned, licensed, or managed hardware and software,
University assigned user accounts, and use of the university network via a physical or wireless
connection, regardless of the ownership of the computer or device connected to the network.

These policies also apply to personally owned computers and devices connected by wire or wireless
to the University network, and to off-site computers that connect remotely to the University’s
network services.

" Protect their user-ids and system from unauthorized use. Everyone is responsible for all
access to university information resources and technology by their user-ids or any activity
originating from their system. An individual’s user-ids and Password act together as their
electronic signature.

Contingency Planning

Managers and administrators of Confidential systems are responsible for creating, implementing,
and testing, at least biannually, disaster recovers and business continuity plans for those systems.
All plans must address and document the following:

= An analysis of data criticality, which considers importance relative to the mission of the

Confidential data on the system.

= Data backup policies and procedures.

= Recovery procedures used to restore operational capacity or data.

= Emergency operating plans, which detail the process for maintaining business operations
and protecting Confidential data while operating in an emergency mode.

= Procedures for testing and revising emergency operating plans. Copies of these plans should
be stored in multiple secure locations, including off-site.

¢ Any relevant regulatory, compliance, or policy changes.

Policy Overview

This policy is internal to Bennett University Domain which defines an anti-virus policy on
every computer including how often a virus scan is done, how often updates are done, and
what programs will be used to detect, prevent, and remove malware programs. It defines
what types of files attachments are blocked at the mail server and the frequency and
approach of scanning.

Scope

This policy is designed to protect Bennett University supported by the Office of
Information Technology against intrusion by viruses and other malware.

Explanatory Notes

AV policy is designed to help prevent infection of Bennett University BCCL computers,
networks, and technology systems by computer viruses and other malicious code.

Anti-Virus Policy

Office of Information Technology will ensure all client machines and servers with Windows
and Linux operating systems will have anti-virus software as well as a scanning engine
running in real-time.

= The anti-virus software will operate in real-time on all servers and client computers.
An Anti-virus client must be configured for real-time protection.

The anti-virus is a new generation tool that should block all types of vulnerable
scripts/processes.

The real-time protection must cover email attachments, downloads, storage
medium, network file-sharing services, web Pages, and other possible identifiable
sources of malware.

Any application-specific exclusion based on OEM recommendations, should be

requirements should be accommodated on an individual basis.

Scope

Data custodians are responsible for providing adequate backups to ensure the recovery of
electronic information (including UI Records and software) in the event of failure. These backup
provisions will allow University business processes, including the research enterprise to be
resumed in a reasonable amount of time with minimal loss of data. Since failures can take many
forms and may occur over time, multiple generations of backups should be maintained.

Federal and state regulations pertaining to the long-term retention of information (e.g., financial
records) will be met using separate archive policy and procedures, as determined by the Business
Owner of the information, and in accord with the Records Management Program. Long-term
archive requirements are beyond the scope of this policy.

infrastructure and systems (IT assets) that are used by students, staff, researchers, and academics.

informal, for their use within teams and across IT Services and the University. This Policy outlines the

practice.

Purpose

This policy establishes the Information Technology (IT) Disaster Recovery (DR) guideline for Bennett
University (BU) and is designed to assist in executing recovery processes in response to a disaster or
significant IT disruption.

Scope
This Policy applies to all the university IT resources and connected systems.

Policy Statement

This policy is implemented to minimize the impact of significant incidents on BU’s IT services and recover
from the unavailability of IT systems to an acceptable level through a combination of responsive and
recovery controls.

Attacks that are judged to be minor in scope or short term in duration, and originate inside
the University, will be validated and if confirmed, reported to the appropriate judicial
representative after one warning from the Information Security Officer. The warning to the
source explains that they are in violation of the University's Acceptable Use of Information
Technology Resources Policy, and are being given one chance to modify their behavior. If the
initial attack is relatively more serious, yet still "minor", the warning is to be waived and a
report made to the appropriate judicial representative. This is a judgment call to be made by
the Information Security Officer.

A judicial report will result in a permanent record of the attack, and a sanction(s)
commensurate to the seriousness of the attack. The intent is to provide an opportunity for
members of our community to learn that we take these matters seriously and will not
overlook inappropriate and potentially damaging behavior. Repeated attacks will result in
escalation to policy regarding incidents having long term and/or major damage.

Attacks which originate outside the University will be reported to the appropriate service
provider by the Information Security Officer if of sufficient seriousness to warrant action on
their part. The service provider will be given detail regarding the attack in order that the
attacker may be dealt with according to the service provider's terms of use. It is not
economically feasible for the University to pursue additional action against attackers (or
their service provider) for minor problems.

When the source of a minor attack cannot be determined, because of a lack of evidence or
because of faulty evidence, then it is in the best interest of the University to close the issue.
(Evidence may be in the form of system recording (log) facilities, monitors, cache files,
program dumps, network traces, disk storage media, etc.)

Long Term Attack and/or with Major Damage

* In consultation with the Information Security Officer, once the entity responsible for the
system or network determines that an attack is of "major" consequence or damage, or the
attack continues for a long duration (on-going or greater than one day), operational steps
must be taken to preserve evidence. Major damage might be a loss (or corruption) of
institutional data, an extended outage of a critical service or application, or other high-
impact/high-cost damage.

An on-going attack originating inside the University will be reported to appropriate campus
service providers as soon as it is detected. If needed, that group will perform tracing through
network analysis to pinpoint the source of the attack. Alternatively, if the attack is detected
through networking analysis, it will be reported to the Information Security Officer and the
entity responsible for the system as soon as possible after its detection.

Introduction:

Bennett University recognizes the increasing demand for flexibility in the modern workplace, where
employees may use personal devices for work-related activities. This Personal Device Usage Policy
establishes guidelines and expectations for employees accessing Bennett University's official
emails and resources through personal devices.

As technology evolves, so does the need for a secure and standardized approach to personal device
usage. This policy aims to strike a balance between providing employees with the flexibility to use
personal devices and maintaining the security, confidentiality, and integrity of our company's data.

Purpose:

This policy is established to govern the use of personal devices for accessing company resources,
ensuring the security, confidentiality, and integrity of Bennett University's data. The policy outlines
acceptable use, security requirements, and employee responsibilities when utilizing personal
devices to access official emails and other resources.

Scope:

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who access Bennett
University's official emails and resources through personal devices.

Acceptable Use:

Employees are permitted to use personal devices for work-related activities. However,
these devices must be used in compliance with Bennett University's policies and guidelines.
Prohibited activities include but are not limited to:

* Unauthorized access or use of company data.

* Sharing company credentials or sensitive information.
* Use of personal devices for non-work-related activities during working hours.

To ensure the security of company data, personal devices used to access company
resources must meet the following minimum requirements:

SOP - Employee Last Day IT Handover

Introduction This SOP outlines the procedures that need to be followed when an employee leaves the
organization and requires an IT handover.

Responsibilities The following responsibilities will be assigned to specific individuals:

* HR Manager: Initiates the process of employee offboarding, including IT handover.
* IT Manager: Ensures that all IT assets assigned to the employee are returned and data is
secured.
Employee: Provides all IT assets assigned to them and cooperates with the IT Manager during
the handover process.
IT Handover Process

Step 1: HR Manager notifies IT Manager Once an employee submits their resignation letter, the HR
Manager will notify the IT Manager about the employee's last day.

Step 2: IT Manager schedules the handover The IT Manager will schedule a meeting with the employee on
their last day to perform the IT handover. The meeting should be conducted in person, and both parties
should sign off on the handover form.

Step 3: Employee returns IT assets The employee will return all IT assets assigned to them, including
laptops, mobile phones, tablets, and any other IT equipment. The IT Manager will check the assets to
ensure that they are in good condition and functioning properly.

Step 4: Data backup and deletion The IT Manager will ensure that all data on the employee's IT assets is
backed up and stored securely. The data will be deleted from the assets and any cloud storage platforms
that the employee had access to.

Step 5: Account deactivation The IT Manager will deactivate all accounts that the employee had access
to, including email, software, and cloud storage accounts. This will ensure that the employee no longer
has access to any sensitive information.

Step 6: Handover form sign off Both the employee and the IT Manager will sign off on the handover form,
indicating that all IT assets have been returned and all necessary steps have been taken to secure data.

Conclusion By following this SOP, the organization can ensure that all IT assets are returned, data is
secured, and sensitive information is protected. This will help the organization to maintain its integrity
and reputation.
