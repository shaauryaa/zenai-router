About Bennett University

An Overview

Bennett University has been set up by the Bennett Coleman and Company Limited — better known as Times Group -
India’s largest media house. Bennett University embodies the spirit of 175-year old group’s culture of leadership and
service to the Nation. Times Group has continually undertaken initiatives for the betterment of Indian society by
extending the core journalistic principles of Trust, Knowledge and Public Service. Times Group runs several
educational initiatives such as Teach India and Newspaper in Education.

Bennett University is established at Greater NOIDA in the state of Uttar Pradesh by an Act of the State Legislature of
Uttar Pradesh (U.P. Act 24/2016) as a private university empowered to grant degrees as specified under section 22
of the UGC Act of Government of India. The University aims to provide quality education at undergraduate and
postgraduate levels to make the students ready for professional careers.

The University runs four independent schools offering degree-level education in fields of engineering, applied
sciences, journalism and media management, law, and business management. The University fosters a proactive
environment of innovation and entrepreneurship, while enhancing skills in all areas of higher education through the
internationally acclaimed centers of excellence such as Centre for Innovation & Entrepreneurship (CIE) and Centre
of Executive Education (CEE). Hatchery — the incubation center run by CIE is recognized under the U.P. State Start-
up Policy.

Vision

To become a Centre of Excellence comparable to the best in the world; helping define aspirations
and possibilities for the long term and producing ‘life and career ready’ professionals with
leadership qualities, who are ready to challenge and be challenged -in the areas of technology,
innovation, management and entrepreneurship.

Campus Life

Office of Student Affairs

A large residential student population on the campus creates its own complex sociology. At Bennett University, the
social experience outside of the classroom and academics is considered as an integral element of the professional
development of the student. The Office of Student Affairs (SA) at the University focuses on creating a campus
environment conducive to the overall goals of the University. SA attends to key aspects of residential campus life
and the non-academic pursuits of the students. SA strives to enhance the potential of the students. It seeks to
foster a social environment that is pleasant and productive for the student, where the student discovers his/her
potential and is motivated to strive towards being a competent professional and a good human being invested with
ethical standing and sound character. SA staff is guided by the following five principles:

Ethical integrity:
In performing its tasks and actions

Actively engage & collaborate:
With students, staff, faculty and management

Be respectful:
Towards all

Driven by evidence:
Guided by In adjudicating on disciplinary matters, and in recognizing student achievements; guided by policy
frameworks

Agility:
In front-facing and resolving situations and grievances at any time of the day

The scope of SA includes student living, dining, cultural activities, sports, health, and planning and execution of
major University-level events. It is involved in setting processes and structures for delivery of key services to the
students and maintaining discipline on the campus. It is an integral part of the University and works closely with the
faculty and administrative arms of the University to ensure the welfare of the student community.

Living on the Campus

The student community at Bennett University reflects the diversity of the nation, drawing students from all parts of

India. The campus life induces an appreciation of this diversity and engenders a spirit of mutual respect and shared
living within the students. Student develop life-long friendships that cut across the linguistic and cultural barriers.

|. Hostels

The University promotes shared living and learning by operating all hostels on a room-sharing basis (double or triple
occupancy). Hostels are gender-segregated, fully air-conditioned and equipped with the basic amenities. Every
student is allotted:

* Bed and mattress

* Study table and chair
* Cupboard and dust bin
+ Wi-Fi connectivity

Every student is responsible to maintain the University-issued property in good condition. In case of loss,
vandalism, or exceptionable damage to the University property, the student will be liable to pay charges including
fines as may be prescribed under the policies. Each hostel has acommon room on the ground floor equipped with a
television set and recreational sports equipment such as snooker table, table tennis, and board games.

Hostel rooms are allotted to the students for each academic year commencing in the month of July after
completion of registration formalities. The allocation policies are designed to achieve a balance between the twin
objectives of complementarity and diversity and may be changed from time to time. Once allocated, the student will
not be allowed to change his/her room without the warden’s permission.

Dining Services

Resident students are served with fresh cooked vegetarian meals in the common dining areas of Blocks B and N.
They are entitled to breakfast, lunch, evening snacks and dinner in the dining areas. Every resident student must
register and obtain a food card, or any other form of identification issued by the University for this purpose. All
resident students must produce their issued card(s) before availing the meal services.

The dining facility is available for the hostel residents free of charge. Day scholars who have not availed of the hostel
facilities may dine after paying appropriate charges. The dining hours are:

Breakfast 7:00 am to 9:30 am
Lunch 12:00 noon to 2:00 pm
Evening Snacks 5:30 pm to 7:00 pm

Dinner 8:00 pm to 10:00 pm

Apart from the regular meals in the cafeteria of Blocks B and N, the University has established special eateries who
serve snacks and other items at affordable prices.

The Mess and Dorm Committee is a student-driven committee and a part of the student council. The committee
works with the students to receive suggestions and to improve the services to the students. The weekly meals
menu is decided through regular meetings between the committee and the service provider. The menu is posted at
the cafeteria and circulated through social media channels. The students can also approach the committee or the
office of Student Affairs for any grievances or suggestions.

Wellness and Health

The University's Wellness Center has a well-equipped health centre with four beds, and primary care facilities
attended round-the-clock by experienced male and female nurses. A qualified general physician is available during
the daytime, and on call in the night. The University has an arrangement with the Kailash Hospital, the nearest multi-
speciality healthcare facility. Emergency cases or students requiring special care are taken to Kailash Hospital in an
ambulance, which is permanently stationed in campus and operates on a 24x7 basis.

All important medical cases needing specific attention are reported to the Wardens, Student Affairs, and to
parents/local guardians through voice calls. For prolonged medical illness or in case of infection, recovery rooms (if
required) are available. The center can be accessed on helpline no: 0120-7199500.

The center regularly organizes health checkup camps, blood donation drives, and physiotherapy sessions for
students and staff.

|. Counselling

The University engages the services of a clinical psychologist to provide counselling services to students. The
Counselling Services follow strict protocols of ensuring confidentiality of information shared during the
counselling process. Students who wish to avail the services of the counsellor should directly approach the
counsellor at the Wellness Center.

Il. Medical Insurance

The University provides Rs.1 lakh cashless medical insurance to students through Oriental Insurance Company
Limited. For details, students can get in touch with the Human Resource department or the Student Affairs office.

|. Late Entry to Campus

Student movement records are reviewed at 20:00 hours by the wardens for any anomalies such as students not
having returned back after the expiry of their Out-passes, or day scholars who have stayed back on the campus etc.
Each such anomalous caseis investigated through calls to the students and/or their parents/guardians.

No student will be allowed to enter the college campus after 22:00 hours. If the arriving Train/Flight/Bus is delayed,
students are advised to stay at the Airport/Railway/Bus station lounge in the interest of their safety. Travelling on
roads in the night is not recommended in the interest of safety of the student.

Inthe case of an emergency, students must inform their respective wardens and parents.

In case of special exigencies, late entry may be allowed. In such cases, an email is sent to the student asking to
explain the reason for his/her delay with a copy to his/her parents/local guardians. Genuine cases are treated with
compassion. Incase the late entry is of indiscipline, the parents, SA office, the Head of Administration and the Chief
Security Officer are notified by email.

IL Student search and frisking

To check the entry of prohibited items into the campus, all students entering the campus are physically frisked at
the main entrance gate and their belongings are searched for any prohibited items.

The search and frisking procedures apply to each entry of a student. Male students are searched by male security
guards. Female students are checked by female security guards within an enclosed space at the gate. In case a
student is found to be in possession of prohibited items, the same is recorded in an incident form and a photograph
of the student along with the substance is taken as evidence for further actions. A detailed report of the incident is
forwarded to the University's Proctorial Committee for further actions.

Ill. Personal vehicles

Students are not allowed to bring their personal vehicles inside the campus. All such vehicles must be parked
outside the main gate at the students’ own risk. The University is not be responsible for any damage or theft of the
vehicle. Day scholars may park their vehicles only at the designated spaces outside the Campus at their own risk.
Resident students will not be allowed to park any vehicles at the spaces designated for day scholars.

Career Services Center

Bennett University aims to impart holistic education with the focus on equipping students with skills that will be
relevant in the changing economy. Preparing students for employability is an integral component of this approach.
Career Services Center (CSC) employs several strategies towards this goal.

I. Industry in the Classroom

An ‘Industry in the Classroom’ combines practice orientation along with enhancements in problem-solving skills of
a student. Under this approach, students work on real-life situational projects that include on-site learning and
exposure to planning resources and execution. The University has created a few platforms ro impart learning:

Bennett CXO Series: Acting as an informational bridge between the industry and the student.

Industry Insights Programme: To sensitize students about the intricacies of working across multiple industries and
explore diverse career opportunities with Industry practitioners sharing key insights on working in specific
industries.

Employability Development Programs: These include experiential Learning Programmes

Conferences: Between the industry and the Academia.

Il. Classroom to Industry

CSC organizes periodic visits to companies, where the students gain real-world exposure to industrial operations. It
is a great opportunity for students to relate the classroom-learning with workplace practices.

Ill. Bennett Finishing School

At Bennett University, we believe that students who are trained well can be placed well. CSC working with student-
led placement committees spearhead the placement outreach activities for all programmes.

Bennett Finishing School (BFS) is an intiative to enhance the student employability. BFS organizes several training
programs suchas:

Industry Insights Sessions: With industry practitioners helping students with key insights to explore diverse career
opportunities

Life Skills Training: To build self-confidence, oral communication skills, group effectiveness, problem solving skills,
decision making skills etc.

Improving the profile on paper and on social media: Include writing skills, Resume and covering letter development,
Video resumes, Curating the social media presence on sites such as LinkedIn.

Networking skills: Making connections for improving the student's opportunities
Presentation skills: Include interview training through mock interviews, case studies, presentability etc.

Preparing for competitive examinations: On campus coaching facility in competitive examinations such as GRE,
GMAT etc., on self-funded basis for students wishing to go abroad for further studies.

IV. Internships & Placements

In today’s competitive job market an internship or placement can have tremendous impact on a student's career.
Employers are increasingly using their internship schemes as a major recruitment channel for their graduate intake
programs. CSC offers strong support for student internships and placements by reaching out to corporates and
employers from different sectors.

Information Technology Services

The University’s Information Technologies Office provides students with a wide range of computing resources,
including email, networked information, interactive computing, campus technology support, Internet service,
academic and learning computing and printing services.

Campus technology is meant for communication, research, information repositories, and other specific purposes of
the University. Students are expected to utilize these resources in a responsible manner and in accordance with the
guidelines set forth by the University.

Additional information and specific guidelines regarding the acceptable use of technology resources are available
on the University's intranet website. For queries concerning the acceptable use of IT, students should consult the
representative of the Information technology Services office.

I. Accessing the campus systems

Bennett University provides a username to all enrolled students. The student ID or NetID is allocated at the time of
the admission. The username is usually based on the first letters of the students’ first and last names respectively
followed by four digits. For example:

Uttam Garg may receive NetID Ug7282 | ManviJainmayreceive NetID Mj9233

The email ID follows the nomenclature NetID@bennett.edu.in. Login credentials can be used to connect to the
campus Wi-Fi network, to access official emails, learning management system, ERP mobile application and various
other applications.

Il. NetID and passwords

All students receive their unique NetID and password at the time of registration. Students must not share these
credentials with anyone as per the University’s Information Security Policy. Some of the security practices include:

+ Changing passwords after first log-in.

* Changing passwords every 90 days.

¢ Avoid passwords that could be easily hacked such as names, DOB, consecutive number sequences etc.
* Logging off from the systems after completing the work

* Enabling lock screen features with password to unlock

In case password is lost, forgotten or compromised, students should visit the IT service desk team to receive new
passwords. For security reasons, the IT service desk will not give new passwords over the phone or by email.

IIL. Internet facilities and network access

The University has access to adequate network bandwidth to meet the needs of a growing campus. The campus is
Wi-Fi enabled and students can access printers, the Internet, Learning Management System (LMS) mobile
application and other applications from anywhere in the campus. Some of the LAB's and specified areas are
ethernet-enabled. Students may use their NetID to access secure network in the study areas, common areas, hostel
rooms or the libraries.

There are no restrictions on the use of internet bandwidth and/or data downloads for valid academic purposes.
Large consumption of bandwidth or data will be permitted upon approvals from the respective faculty.

IV. Bennett Mobile Plan

The University has negotiated with the telecom carriers a special Bennett Mobile 4G data plan which offers large
data pack with rollover features at highly discounted rates for the students and staff. This plan is not available in
open market. Students may wish to avail of this plan for their non-academic usage. For more information on how to
subscribe to Bennett Mobile Plan, please contact ops-helpdesk@bennett.edu.in, or 0120-719-9494.

Behavioural Expectations and Policies

The University has several policies in place to ensure that students behave in a responsible manner aligned to the
objectives of the University and towards creating a safe and conducive environment on the campus. Some of the
relevant policies are briefly listed below for the information of the students. For more detailed policy statements,
students should visit the University website or approach the Office of the Registrar.

Code on decorum in public spaces

All the students are cautioned to be socially sensitive in their dressing styles and follow norms of socially
acceptable behaviors in public spaces. Public space may be described as any space on the campus where the
student can be seen and/or heard by those unknown to him/her. This caution is especially applicable when
students across genders get together. Students should be aware that in the times of HD camera devices and fast
internet, their pictures, videos or sounds can go viral quickly and can fall into wrong hands, often with unpleasant
consequences for them.

Anti-Ragging Policy

Ragging in any form is unlawful and strictly prohibited. Any student found ragging shall be expelled from the
University and FIRs lodged against him/her as per orders of the Honorable Supreme Court. These rules have been
formulated in consonance with UGC Rules 2016, on Anti ragging in Higher Educational Institutions/Universities, as
published in the gazette notification. The University has implemented systems and procedures to actively comply
with the orders of the Honorable Supreme Court and the UGC Rules of 2016.

For more details on the Anti-ragging Policy, please visit:
https://www.Bennett.edu.in/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/2Anti-Ragging-Policy-and-Ctte-Nov-18.pdf

Policy on prevention of sexual harassment

The University is committed to treating every student/employee/contractor with dignity and respect. The University
fully complies with 'The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition & Redressal) Act,
2013' passed by the Government of India, and is committed to create a work environment that is free from sexual
harassment of any kind, whether verbal, physical or visual. The University policy provides guidelines for prompt
redressal of complaints related to sexual harassment including expulsion/termination of the offender and/or filing
appropriate FIRs for the offense.

For more information on the Prevention of Sexual Harassment Policy, Please visit:

Policy against damage and vandalism

Students must take good care of all University property — whether issued to them or not — and must refrain from
acts of vandalism, defacing buildings, painting graffiti on the walls, roads, furniture and fittings etc. in any manner.
Any damage to University property, whether willful or otherwise shall be viewed as indiscipline and will invite
disciplinary actions. In addition, such students will be required to reimburse the loss to the University through
financial fines equal to not less than twice the cost of replacement or repairs. In case of group vandalism, each

Day Scholar's Policy

Normally, the University provides hostel accommodation and dining facilities to all students. University may, on a
discretionary basis allow students to stay off-campus while pursuing the academic programs. Such non-residential
students are called day scholars. Day scholars are permitted to access all academic facilities such as the
classrooms, laboratories, library, computer facilities etc., sports and cultural facilities, and sports center. They may,
on a paid basis access and use the dining/cafeteria and transport services. However, day scholars are cannot
access or stay in the hostels and cannot use laundry facilities. Those found violating these restrictions will face
stringent penalties.

Visitors Policy

Only parents will be allowed to visit the students on the campus after the prior approval of the warden. Parents are
advised to avoid visiting during the class hours. In case of emergency, parents can directly visit the student after
informing the warden. In case someone other than parents wishes to meet the student, the parents should inform
the warden a day in advance of such a visit. Students can meet visitors only in the common area such as the
cafeteria, reception, etc. Students are strongly advised NOT to meet visitors in the Car Parking Area or any other
isolated areas. Violation of this may lead to disciplinary action.

Discipline Policy

The welfare and discipline of students are two integral parts of an Institutional behavior. Student’s behavior and
discipline will therefore be assessed and will receive the same attention as the academic work. Discipline includes
the observance of good conduct and orderly behaviour by the students of the University. Any violation of the code of
conduct or breach of any rules or regulations of the University by any student shall constitute an act of indiscipline
and shall make him/her liable for disciplinary action. The Vice Chancellor shall consider the report of the standing
proctorial committee and recommendations of Chief Proctor and decide upon the quantum of punishment to be
awarded.
